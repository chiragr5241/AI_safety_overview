Is NVIDIA helping to pay for the demand behind its own sales?

A review of twelve fiscal quarters, Q4 FY2024 to Q3 FY2027, built only from public filings. It traces cash, receivables, investments, debt and guarantees. It is not an audit and makes no finding of misconduct.

AssessmentWhat the report concludesFour areas to test, and what the public record can and cannot show4areas

NVIDIA’s borrowing is modest. What needs closer examination is the support it gives customers, the collection of fast-growing receivables, and its underwriting of future capacity.

EarningsEarnings and cash conversionRevenue rose fivefold in twelve quarters; cash lagged profit by $67.4B over the last year$115.8BOctober revenue

October revenue rose 20.4% on the quarter. Receivables grew 28.5% and inventory 25.8%, absorbing $18.1B and $8.1B of cash. Over the last four quarters operating cash fell $67.4B short of net income; receivables took $47.7B, inventory $20.0B, and $31.0B of investment gains were profit with no cash behind it. These overlap and should not be added. Data centers were about 93% of the latest quarter’s revenue.

Working capitalWorking capital and supplier creditCustomers take longer to pay and inventory sits longer112day cash cycle

Days to collect rose from 38 to 57, and the cash conversion cycle from 80 to 112 days. Inventory at October was $15.8B of raw materials, $17.8B in process and $6.2B finished. Supply commitments rose from $279B in July to $390B, so product transitions and purchase losses deserve stress tests. NVIDIA reports 64 days to collect on its own convention; the report uses one method throughout.

RequestsWhat a regulator should ask forFive tests, ranked by their effect on revenue, cash and contingent losses5tests
  1. Linked transactions and related partiesJoin invoices, payments, investments, guarantees and cloud orders by ultimate owner, project and date. How much revenue depended on money NVIDIA supplied?
  2. Revenue, receivables and cutoffConfirm receivables with customers and lenders; check collections after quarter end, acceptance records, returns and credit notes.
  3. Guarantees and consolidationObtain lender confirmations and valuation models; decide which entities bear losses and whether any should be consolidated.
  4. Procurement and capital payablesTrace vendor invoices to receiving logs and asset registers; look for supplier finance and unrecorded liabilities.
  5. Investments and governanceConfirm holdings with custodians; review round terms, side letters and common counterparties across equity marks and revenue.

Positive operating cash, independently reported contracts and the annual audit count against a claim of wholesale fabrication. None replaces transaction-level checks.
