What this site is for

AI safety, for political scientists

This site explains AI safety to readers who study institutions, power and cooperation, and who have no machine-learning background. For now it has three goals.

How to read this site. Every page starts with a picture and something to try. The full text sits one click behind it, and nothing is left out.

Why this audience: many open questions in AI safety are questions about institutions. Who holds authority over a system, who can monitor it, and who can enforce a commitment once it has been made?

The three goals

  1. 1

    Cover the high-level AI risks

    What could go wrong with advanced AI, and how do the different worries relate to one another?

    The aim is a short account of the main kinds of risk, each with a concrete example, that a reader can follow without maths.

    Read the risks guide
    On the site nowA six-chapter guide to one report
    • When AIs Meet AIs, a six-chapter guide to one research report on what goes wrong when many AI agents deal with one another.
    • It covers three failure modes (miscoordination, conflict and collusion), seven risk factors and 13 case studies.
    Not yetOne overview of every kind of risk
    • A single overview of the main risk types across the whole field. Risks from one AI system acting alone appear only as entries in the field primer.
  2. 2

    Define the terms

    What do people in this field mean by alignment, evaluation, interpretability or scheming?

    The aim is one short, stable definition per term, so that a word means the same thing on every page and a reader can check it quickly.

    Open the field primer
    On the site nowA primer, a glossary and a terms list
    • A field primer of 31 ideas in AI safety, searchable and grouped into five themes.
    • A glossary of 16 terms used in the risks guide.
    • A list of industry and funding terms used in the ecosystem map.
    Not yetOne merged glossary for the site
    • One merged glossary for the whole site. The three lists are separate for now.
  3. 3

    Map the AI industry and its ecosystem

    Who supplies the infrastructure, who pays, who decides, who does the research, who shapes policy, and who bears the consequences?

    The aim is a descriptive map of the organisations involved and the ties between them. Investment, control, grants, commercial contracts and political spending are kept apart.

    Open the ecosystem map
    On the site now100 actors, 105 ties and who decides
    • Who has a stake in AI safety? It covers 100 actors and groups, 105 relationships, 48 financial observations and 93 sources.
    • A section on who decides and what they stand to gain: decision rights, access terms, binding rules and disclosed interests.
    • Public-source evidence runs to 4 October 2026.
    Not yetBudget shares, signed agreements, other countries
    • Each safety organisation's main funders as a share of its budget, the signed agreements between labs and evaluators, current shareholdings, and institutions outside the US and UK. The map marks these gaps where they occur.

HOW THE GOALS ARE MET

Four ground rules

Plain language first

No maths. A technical term is defined where it is first used.

Evidence apart from interpretation

What a source states is kept separate from what this site concludes from it, and each is labelled.

Gaps are stated

Where something is not known, the page says so. An unknown figure is left blank and is never treated as zero.

Unofficial

The site is not affiliated with, or endorsed by, any organisation it describes.
